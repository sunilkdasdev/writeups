Lesson 18: The 8 Fallacies of Distributed Computing

Watch Video Lesson | May 21, 2018

Video Summary

Mark Richards reviews the 8 fallacies of distributed computing - the misconceptions that cause most distributed system failures. These are critical for anyone moving from monolithic to distributed architectures.

  1. The Network is Reliable - Networks fail constantly; plan for partial failures
  2. Latency is Zero - Network calls are slow (ms vs ns); don't assume instant communication
  3. Bandwidth is Infinite - Network congestion happens; optimize payloads
  4. The Network is Secure - Assume hostile network; encrypt and authenticate
  5. Topology Doesn't Change - Networks evolve; use service discovery
  6. There is One Administrator - Multiple teams/systems; coordinate changes
  7. Transport Cost is Zero - Resources cost money; budget for infrastructure
  8. The Network is Homogeneous - Heterogeneous systems exist; support diversity
